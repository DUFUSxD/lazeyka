Privacy policy

Effective October 20, 2026. Applies to the Lazeyka extension for Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers, and to its installer.

In short

Lazeyka has no servers, accounts, analytics or ads of its own. Everything you enter stays on your computer. The developer receives none of it.

What is stored and where

  • Subscription links and their names, your site lists, chosen servers and settings — in the extension's browser storage (chrome.storage).
  • The secret — the password the extension uses to control the core — in the browser and in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Lazeyka\core\config.yaml.
  • A device ID — a random number generated on this computer (not tied to hardware or accounts) — in the extension's storage.
  • Data from your subscription provider (name, support and account links, announcements) — in the extension's storage, if you allowed fetching it.

The settings string you copy yourself contains your subscription links and site lists; it goes nowhere unless you send it yourself.

What the extension connects to

  • The mihomo core on this computer (127.0.0.1) — to pass it your subscriptions and rules and read server status.
  • Your subscription sites (your provider's panels). The core downloads the subscription; the request carries the random device ID, “Windows” and “Lazeyka”, which panels with a device limit need. If you clicked “Allow”, the extension also requests the subscription itself to read the provider's name, links and announcements. Subscription logos load from the same sites.
  • GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com) — checking for a new version and the updated list of sites blocked in Russia. These requests carry none of your data. Update checks can be turned off.
  • Cloudflare: cp.cloudflare.com — checking whether servers are alive (through the servers themselves); speed.cloudflare.com — only when you press “Speed test”, always through the server being tested.

Your traffic

Sites that your rules send via VPN go through the servers in your subscriptions. Those servers belong to your provider or to you — the Lazeyka developer has no access to them; what they do with traffic is up to your provider's policy. Other sites are opened by the browser directly, as without Lazeyka.

What Lazeyka does not do

  • collect statistics, browsing history or information about your computer;
  • share or sell data to third parties;
  • show ads or load executable code from the internet (an extension update is a new version from the open repository).

Deleting your data

Removing the extension deletes its storage. The core and its files are removed with the command in the Uninstall section of the README.

Questions

Lazeyka is open source: github.com/DUFUSxD/lazeyka. Questions — in issues; vulnerabilities — privately, as described in SECURITY.md.
